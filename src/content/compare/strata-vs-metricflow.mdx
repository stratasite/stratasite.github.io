title: Strata vs dbt Semantic Layer (MetricFlow)
competitor: dbt Semantic Layer
category: semantic-layer
summary: The closest peer on the semantic side. MetricFlow links entities by name inside a dbt project and serves metrics to other tools. Strata conforms by name across the whole warehouse, adds five measure types and federation, and is the self service layer itself.
order: 10

import VersusSection from '../../components/VersusSection.astro';

<VersusSection slug={props.slug} competitor={props.competitor} theme="modeling">

MetricFlow is the closest thing to Strata's modeling idea. Semantic models
declare entities, dimensions, and measures as YAML in the dbt project, entities
with the same name link models together, and a metric across models is
aggregated per model and then joined. That is the right shape.

The difference is how much of it you author and how far it reaches. In
MetricFlow every model carries its own entity declarations, join rules, and
metric specs, and correctness across models depends on those being right. Strata
derives compatibility from the registry: give a concept the same name across
facts and it is conformed, give distinct concepts distinct names and they stay
apart, and blending groups handle the cases where names genuinely differ. Any
combination the model cannot answer is refused before execution. There is no
path to a wrong number, and no combination you forgot to declare.

</VersusSection>

<VersusSection slug={props.slug} competitor={props.competitor} theme="measures">

MetricFlow's metric types, simple, ratio, cumulative, derived, and conversion,
plus non additive dimensions for semi additive measures, cover a lot. What they
do not cover is level of detail: a share of total, a fixed denominator, or a
hierarchy excluded from the group by has to be built as a second metric and a
ratio.

Strata ships Standard, Complex, Snapshot, LOD, and ad hoc measures, with one
click year over year, moving average, and percent of total, and segments at the
measure level so a cohort sits beside the baseline in one view. The domain
expert builds these in the editor; nobody opens a YAML file.

</VersusSection>

<VersusSection slug={props.slug} competitor={props.competitor} theme="operator">

MetricFlow's operator is the analytics engineer. Consumers reach the metrics
through a BI tool, a notebook, or an API, and the experience they get is
whichever tool sits on top. There is no self service surface of its own.

Strata is the consumption layer. Non technical domain experts build reports,
dashboards, agents, and exports against the governed model, with per keystroke
validation and a layout engine, and engineers keep the model current. The
guarantees travel with the retrieval interface, which is why Strata never
exposes the model to a foreign query builder.

</VersusSection>

<VersusSection slug={props.slug} competitor={props.competitor} theme="performance">

MetricFlow compiles to SQL and runs it on the warehouse. Every dashboard
refresh and every agent turn is warehouse compute, and latency is whatever the
warehouse gives you.

Strata is aggregate aware and federates across OLAP engines you own, with
partition aware routing to a ClickHouse or Druid hot tier when the filters fit
and a fallback to the warehouse when they do not. One measure definition serves
every engine.

</VersusSection>

<VersusSection slug={props.slug} competitor={props.competitor} theme="ai">

dbt is building agent access through an MCP server and its APIs, and the
governed metric definitions help. But the retrieval experience is yours to
build, and an agent that composes a bad request gets an error rather than a
guided alternative.

Strata's validation loop is the agent API. An agent proposes a partial query,
gets the compatible next steps and structured refusals, and lands on a correct
answer the same way a person does.

</VersusSection>

<VersusSection slug={props.slug} competitor={props.competitor} theme="better">

If your metrics belong next to the models that build them, in the same repo,
the same pull requests, and the same CI, MetricFlow is the natural fit, and
Strata is a separate project that sits on your dbt built marts. If your
consumers are already in Tableau, Hex, or Mode and you want a headless layer
feeding them over JDBC or GraphQL, MetricFlow does that and Strata does not, on
purpose.

Strata earns its place when the people asking questions cannot write the query,
when the warehouse spans many fact domains with partial conformance, and when
agents need correct answers at sub second latency without burning warehouse
credits.

</VersusSection>
