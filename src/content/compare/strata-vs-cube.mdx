This page condenses our hands on evaluation. The full write up, with the model
and the generated SQL, is in the
[full review of Cube](https://blog.strata.do/p/full-review-of-cube) on the Strata blog.

<VersusSection slug={props.slug} competitor={props.competitor} theme="modeling">

In Cube, cross-fact analysis lives in views, and you need a view for every
combination of facts you want to blend. That guarantees view sprawl and a
standing maintenance burden, and it turns the non-technical user into a
supplicant: "which view has the measures and dimensions I need?" Mix grains
without the right view and you hit an unresolvable join path.

Strata blends automatically. Give the same concept the same name and Strata
aggregates each fact to a common grain, then outer joins on the conformed keys.
No view to author, no combination to pre-build, and invalid blends are refused
before they run, with an explanation and the nearest valid alternative.

</VersusSection>

<VersusSection slug={props.slug} competitor={props.competitor} theme="measures">

Simple measures are easy in both tools. But a semi-additive measure, a monthly
inventory balance, took undocumented multi-stage workarounds in Cube and
generated well over a hundred lines of SQL for a single number (the
[review](https://blog.strata.do/p/full-review-of-cube) shows the model and the
SQL), and calculated measures can reference other measures but not dimensions.
As you layer on complexity the generated SQL only gets harder to debug.

Strata ships five measure types, Standard, Complex, Snapshot, LOD, and ad hoc.
Snapshot is a first-class declaration rather than a workaround, and Complex
measures reference both measures and dimensions. One click adds year over year,
moving average, or percent of total, and a segment on a single measure puts a
cohort beside the baseline in one view.

</VersusSection>

<VersusSection slug={props.slug} competitor={props.competitor} theme="operator">

The primary operator is the person who can drive the full tool: build reports,
dashboards, agents, and exports, not just view them. It is the first question to
settle when choosing an analytics platform. Cube is built for the engineer.
Non-technical users stay passive consumers who depend on engineers to author the
right view.

In Strata the primary operator is the non-technical user. Engineers set the
project up and keep it current as KPIs change and new tables land, but that
upkeep is light. The day-to-day building is done by domain experts.

Cube's dashboards are cloud-only, built through a click-to-place UI that feels
dated next to modern drop zones, and laid out by manual snap-to-grid. Most
telling, they offer no guardrails: a user can assemble an illegal query and only
discover it through a "can't find join path" error. Strata is beautiful by
default, arranges views with a layout engine, and validates every query as it is
built, so a non-technical user cannot construct a broken view in the first place.

</VersusSection>

<VersusSection slug={props.slug} competitor={props.competitor} theme="performance">

Cube's speed comes from Cube Store, an OLAP store built for Cube. It is fast,
and it exists to serve Cube: you cannot point your other tools at it or swap it
for a better engine when one ships.

Strata's performance is aggregate-aware and federated across OLAP engines you
bring and own, ClickHouse, Druid, whatever fits, with partition-aware routing to
the fastest source that can answer. One measure definition serves every engine,
your other tools can query those engines directly over SQL, and the aggregation
and caching work you invest carries over to any tool you add later.

</VersusSection>

<VersusSection slug={props.slug} competitor={props.competitor} theme="ai">

Cube's built-in chat is a bright spot, but its quality is capped by the quality
of your views: it inherits the semantic layer's limitations, and there is no way
to bring your own model.

Strata's validation loop is the agent API. Agents ask broad questions against
the governed model and get the same grain-safe refusals and correct answers a
person does, so agent quality rises with the model instead of being gated by a
hand-built view for every question.

</VersusSection>

<VersusSection slug={props.slug} competitor={props.competitor} theme="better">

Cube is a genuinely capable semantic layer, and it is moving fast. Model
definition in YAML is intuitive, the open-source Docker build iterates in
seconds, and AI-assisted modeling can scaffold dozens of tables in one sitting.
Pre-aggregations are the standout: Cube Store answers a single chart in the low
hundreds of milliseconds and refreshes a multi-chart dashboard in about a
second, even over billions of rows.

If your consumers live in Power BI, Tableau, or Excel and you want a headless
layer feeding them, Cube does that and Strata does not, on purpose. If your
operators are engineers and you would rather not run an OLAP engine, Cube Store
is managed for you. Our
[review of Cube](https://blog.strata.do/p/full-review-of-cube) ends on the
right question: which persona are you trying to enable? Cube enables the
engineer, and does it well. Strata enables the domain expert, and the agent, on
the same governed, grain-safe, federated model.

</VersusSection>
