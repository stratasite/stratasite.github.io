## Where Cube Is Strong

Cube is a genuinely capable semantic layer, and it is moving fast. Model
definition in YAML is intuitive, the open-source Docker build iterates in
seconds (edit a model and it is live on the server, no deploy cycle), and
AI-assisted modeling can scaffold dozens of tables in one sitting.
Pre-aggregations are the standout: Cube Store answers a single chart in the low
hundreds of milliseconds and refreshes a multi-chart dashboard in about a
second, even over billions of rows. On top of that it has grown a BI layer,
cloud workbooks, and a built-in AI chat that probes dimension values to
disambiguate a question. For a technical team it is a strong, well-built
product.

<Callout type="tip" title="Who is the Primary Operator?">
The primary operator is the person who can drive the full tool: build reports,
dashboards, agents, and exports, not just view them. It is the first question to
settle when choosing an analytics platform.

**Who are you trying to enable?**
  1. Technical users (analytics and data engineers) who deliver dashboards and views, or
  2. Non-technical domain experts who need to self-serve analytics.

Cube is built for the first. Non-technical users stay passive consumers who
depend on engineers to author the right view. In Strata the primary operator is
the non-technical user. Engineers set the project up and keep it current as KPIs
change and new tables land, but that upkeep is light. The day-to-day building is
done by domain experts.
</Callout>

## Where Strata Pulls Ahead

**A view for every combination.** In Cube, cross-fact analysis lives in views,
and you need a view for every combination of facts you want to blend. That
guarantees view sprawl and a standing maintenance burden, and it turns the
non-technical user into a supplicant: "which view has the measures and
dimensions I need?" Mix grains without the right view and you hit an
unresolvable join path. Strata blends automatically. Give the same concept the
same name and Strata aggregates each fact to a common grain, then outer-joins on
the conformed keys. No view to author, no combination to pre-build, and invalid
blends are refused before they run, with an explanation and the nearest valid
alternative.

**Hard measures mean hard SQL.** Simple measures are easy in both tools. But a
semi-additive measure, a monthly inventory balance, took undocumented
multi-stage workarounds in Cube and generated well over a hundred lines of SQL
for a single number, and calculated measures can reference other measures but
not dimensions. As you layer on complexity the generated SQL only gets harder to
debug. Strata ships five measure types, Standard, Complex, Snapshot, LOD, and
ad-hoc. Snapshot is a first-class declaration rather than a workaround, and
Complex measures reference both measures and dimensions. One click adds
year-over-year, moving average, or percent of total.

**Dashboards that guide the user, not trap them.** Cube's dashboards are
cloud-only, built through a click-to-place UI that feels dated next to modern
drop zones, and laid out by manual snap-to-grid. Most telling, they offer no
guardrails: a user can assemble an illegal query and only discover it through a
"can't find join path" error. Strata is beautiful by default, arranges views
with a layout engine, and validates every query as it is built, so a
non-technical user cannot construct a broken or wrong-numbered view in the first
place.

**A proprietary cache, versus engines you own.** Cube's speed comes from Cube
Store, a closed OLAP engine. It is fast, but it is a black box you cannot query
with your other tools or replace when something better ships. Strata's
performance is aggregate-aware and federated across OLAP engines you bring and
own, ClickHouse, Druid, whatever fits, with partition-aware routing to the
fastest source that can answer. One measure definition serves every engine, your
other tools can query those engines directly over SQL, and the aggregation and
caching work you invest carries over to any tool you add later.

**AI inherits the layer's limits.** Cube's built-in chat is a bright spot, but
its quality is capped by the quality of your views: it inherits the semantic
layer's limitations, and there is no way to bring your own model. Strata's
validation loop is the agent API. Agents ask broad questions against the
governed model and get the same grain-safe refusals and correct answers a person
does, so agent quality rises with the model instead of being gated by a
hand-built view for every question.

The review of Cube ends on the right question: which persona are you trying to
enable? Cube enables the engineer, and does it well. Strata enables the domain
expert, and the agent, on the same governed, grain-safe, federated model.
