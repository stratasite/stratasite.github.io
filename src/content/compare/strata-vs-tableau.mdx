Tableau models in published data sources and workbooks, and each one carries
its own fields, joins and calculated fields. Every new question tends to spawn
a new data source, redundant measures multiply, and users need a map to find
where to look. The customer service org in our case study hit exactly this:
one Tableau estate, hundreds of data sources, and a per-partner masking
requirement that would have multiplied them ten times over.

Inside one data source, relationships do real work: Tableau aggregates each
table at its own level before combining, so a well built source avoids the
classic fan out. Across data sources, blending joins on linking fields with
limits on what the secondary source can do, and calculated fields, joins and
blends all trust the author. Strata models a whole domain once, in plain YAML
in Git, derives what is legal from the model, and refuses an invalid
combination before it runs. There is no path to a wrong number.

</VersusSection>

<VersusSection slug={props.slug} competitor={props.competitor} theme="measures">

Tableau invented the level of detail expression. FIXED, INCLUDE and EXCLUDE,
plus table calculations, make it possible to express share of total, fixed
denominators and running balances. But each one lives in a workbook or a
published source, written by a Creator, and the same measure ends up defined
five ways in five workbooks.

Strata lifts the idea into the model. Inclusion and Exclusion LOD are measure
types, beside Standard, Complex and Snapshot, defined once and reused by
everyone, with one click year over year, moving average and percent of total.
A segment on a single measure puts a cohort beside the baseline in one view,
which in Tableau is a set plus a calculated field.

</VersusSection>

<VersusSection slug={props.slug} competitor={props.competitor} theme="operator">

The primary operator is the person who can drive the full tool, not just
consume it. In Tableau that person holds a Creator seat: they build the data
source, write the calculations, and lay out the dashboard. Explorers can edit
on the web within what a Creator published, and Viewers consume. The queue
forms in front of the Creators.

In Strata the primary operator is the non-technical user. They start from a
measure, Strata derives the valid paths and validates every keystroke, and
they build reports, dashboards, agents and exports themselves. Tableau's
dashboards are as good as the Creator makes them, pixel by pixel. Strata's are
beautiful by default, arranged by a layout engine.

</VersusSection>

<VersusSection slug={props.slug} competitor={props.competitor} theme="performance">

Tableau is fast on Hyper extracts, which means refresh schedules and numbers
that are as fresh as the last run. Live connections put every interaction on
the warehouse. Either way, speed is a property of extracts or of the warehouse,
not of infrastructure you control.

Strata is aggregate aware and federates across OLAP engines you own, with
partition aware routing to the fastest source that can answer. The warehouse
stays the system of record; a ClickHouse or Druid hot tier takes the repetitive
queries, live. One measure definition serves every engine.

</VersusSection>

<VersusSection slug={props.slug} competitor={props.competitor} theme="ai">

Tableau Pulse delivers insights on metrics defined against a published data
source, and Tableau Agent helps a Creator author. Both inherit the data
source's boundaries: a question that spans sources, or a calculation the
author did not anticipate, is outside what they can answer safely.

Strata's validation loop is the agent API. An agent builds queries through
the same validated interface a person does, across the whole governed model,
and gets the same refusals, so it cannot return a semantically invalid answer.

</VersusSection>

<VersusSection slug={props.slug} competitor={props.competitor} theme="better">

Tableau is the best visualization tool there is. For an analyst who wants to
explore freely, drag anything anywhere, and craft a dashboard to the pixel,
nothing else comes close, and the community, templates and talent pool are
enormous.

If your analysts are the audience, if the sources you care about are files and
SaaS apps rather than a warehouse, or if visual craft is the product, Tableau
does those things and Strata does not. Strata does not connect to Tableau,
on purpose. It augments or replaces it for governed self-service on the
warehouse, which is where the customer service org in our case study took 800
users.

</VersusSection>
