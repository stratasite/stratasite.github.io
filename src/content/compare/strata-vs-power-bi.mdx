<VersusSection slug={props.slug} competitor={props.competitor} theme="modeling">

Every source in Power BI, even a CSV, becomes its own semantic model, and every
use case tends to spawn another one. Git is possible, through Fabric workspace
integration and PBIP project files, but it is opt in and per model, and most
teams still pass .pbix files around. Dozens of models accumulate with no shared
source of truth.

Inside a model, Power BI trusts you. Put Store Sales and Web Sales together and
it will let you slice Web Sales by a Store dimension. The result is the grand
total repeated on every row, with a warning at most. Correctness rests on the
author knowing every legal combination. Strata models a whole domain in a single
semantic layer that lives in Git from the first commit, derives what is legal
from the model, and refuses invalid combinations before they run. There is no
path to a wrong number.

</VersusSection>

<VersusSection slug={props.slug} competitor={props.competitor} theme="measures">

Power BI's expressiveness runs through DAX, which does not map cleanly to SQL
and takes real time to master. Even a semi-additive closing balance needs a
CALCULATE and ENDOFMONTH formula, and the people who can write it are the same
people who are already the bottleneck.

Strata ships five measure types, Standard, Complex, Snapshot, LOD, and ad hoc,
plus one click year over year, moving average, and percent of total, with no new
language to learn. A segment on one measure puts a cohort beside the baseline in
the same view, which in DAX is a filter context exercise per measure.

</VersusSection>

<VersusSection slug={props.slug} competitor={props.competitor} theme="operator">

The primary operator is the person who can drive the full tool, not just consume
it. In Power BI that person is deeply technical: they master a complex UI,
understand the data model, know some SQL, and usually learn DAX. Non-technical
users stay passive, limited to the slicers a report author enabled.

In Strata the primary operator is the non-technical user. They build reports,
dashboards, agents, and exports themselves; engineers set up and lightly
maintain. Dashboard layout in Power BI is pixel perfect and manual, as pretty or
as ugly as the author makes it. Strata is beautiful by default, with a layout
engine that arranges views and validation on every keystroke.

</VersusSection>

<VersusSection slug={props.slug} competitor={props.competitor} theme="performance">

Import, DirectQuery, and Composite are all fast, and all tie you further into
the Microsoft ecosystem. Speed is a property of the Power BI service and the
Vertipaq engine, not of infrastructure you control.

Strata is aggregate aware and federates across OLAP engines you own, with
partition aware routing to the fastest source that can answer. Snowflake stays
the system of record; a ClickHouse or Druid hot tier takes the repetitive
queries. One measure definition serves every engine.

</VersusSection>

<VersusSection slug={props.slug} competitor={props.competitor} theme="ai">

Copilot sits on top of the model and inherits its trust problem: it can write a
DAX measure, but nothing stops it from slicing by an unrelated dimension and
returning a confident wrong total.

Strata's validation loop is the agent API. An agent builds queries through the
same validated interface a person does and gets the same refusals, so it cannot
return a semantically invalid answer.

</VersusSection>

<VersusSection slug={props.slug} competitor={props.competitor} theme="better">

Power BI is genuinely powerful. Signup is frictionless, the connector list is
vast, and data prep, semantic modeling, and report building all live in one
desktop flow. For a Microsoft shop with technical authors, it is a strong end
to end tool, and it is already licensed.

If your consumers live in Teams and Excel, if you need paginated reports for
print or regulators, or if the sources you care about are SaaS apps and files
rather than a warehouse, Power BI does those things and Strata does not. Strata
does not feed Power BI over XMLA either, on purpose. It augments or replaces it
for governed self service on the warehouse.

</VersusSection>
