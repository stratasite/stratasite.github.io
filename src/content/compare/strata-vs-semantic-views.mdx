<VersusSection slug={props.slug} competitor={props.competitor} theme="modeling">

A semantic view declares its tables, its relationships, and its dimensions and
metrics explicitly. Two dimensions with the same name from different tables are
a compile error you fix by renaming one of them. That is the opposite of
Strata's rule, where the shared name is the signal that the two are conformed
and safely blendable, and it matters on a messy warehouse: the naming discipline
you would apply anyway becomes the model, instead of a list of relationships to
keep correct by hand.

Snowflake's blending is join based, so correctness depends on the declared
relationships being right. Strata aggregates each fact to the common grain and
outer joins on conformed keys, never row to row across facts, and refuses any
combination the model cannot answer before it runs. There is no path to a wrong
number.

</VersusSection>

<VersusSection slug={props.slug} competitor={props.competitor} theme="measures">

Semi additive measures are native, through NON ADDITIVE BY, and that is a real
strength. Level of detail is narrower: a window function exclusion covers some
cases, and share of total or a fixed denominator is a second metric.

Strata ships Standard, Complex, Snapshot, LOD, and ad hoc measures, one click
year over year, moving average, and percent of total, and segments at the
measure level so a cohort sits beside the baseline in one view.

</VersusSection>

<VersusSection slug={props.slug} competitor={props.competitor} theme="operator">

A semantic view is authored by a data engineer and consumed through Cortex or a
BI tool. Snowsight is not a self service product, so the experience your
operators get is whichever tool you put on top, and the guarantees flatten at
that boundary.

Strata is the consumption layer. Non technical domain experts build reports,
dashboards, agents, and exports against the governed model, with per keystroke
validation and a layout engine, and the correctness guarantee travels with that
interface into every surface Strata owns, including the Google Sheets add on.

</VersusSection>

<VersusSection slug={props.slug} competitor={props.competitor} theme="performance">

Every query against a semantic view is Snowflake compute. That is simple, and it
is the reason dashboards and agents get slower and more expensive as adoption
deepens.

Strata keeps Snowflake as the system of record and fronts it with a ClickHouse
or Druid hot tier plus aggregate awareness. Partition aware routing sends a
query to the hot tier when the filters fit and to Snowflake when they do not.
One measure definition serves both. Sub second answers and lower Snowflake
spend at the same time.

</VersusSection>

<VersusSection slug={props.slug} competitor={props.competitor} theme="ai">

Cortex Analyst and Cortex Agents make semantic views genuinely agent native,
inside Snowflake. The model gives the agent governed definitions; the answers
still run at warehouse latency, and the retrieval is Snowflake's.

Strata's validation loop is the agent API. Agents get compatible next steps and
structured refusals on every turn, at hot tier latency, and the same absolute
guarantee a person gets: it cannot return a semantically invalid answer.

</VersusSection>

<VersusSection slug={props.slug} competitor={props.competitor} theme="better">

If everything already lives in Snowflake and you want zero new infrastructure,
semantic views are native: governance, row level security, and masking are
inherited from the account, Cortex is in the platform, and the reach into Power
BI and Excel through XMLA is growing. Strata is a server to run, with RLS
declared in the model, and it never feeds a foreign BI tool headlessly.

Strata earns its place when the warehouse is messy and multi domain, when
dashboard and agent latency has become an adoption problem, when Snowflake spend
on repetitive queries needs to come down, and when the people asking questions
need a self service surface rather than a semantic view behind someone else's
tool.

</VersusSection>
