import VersusSection from '../../components/VersusSection.astro';

<VersusSection slug={props.slug} competitor={props.competitor} theme="modeling">

Looker is the closest full stack analogue to Strata: a governed semantic layer
with a BI tool on top. The cost is LookML. Every explore, join, and field is
configured by a developer, and the model grows one explore at a time, each a
hand built statement of what is legal to ask.

Strata derives what is legal from names. Give a concept the same name across
facts and it is conformed; give distinct concepts distinct names and they stay
apart. Compatibility is a property of the registry, not a list you maintain.
Where Looker reaches a second fact through merged results, stitched in the
browser and awkward beyond two, Strata aggregates each fact to the common grain
and outer joins on conformed keys, and refuses anything the model cannot answer.
There is no path to a wrong number.

</VersusSection>

<VersusSection slug={props.slug} competitor={props.competitor} theme="measures">

Looker's measures and table calculations cover the standard cases, and
symmetric aggregates protect against fan out inside an explore. Snapshot and
level of detail measures are the weak spot, and a cohort beside the baseline is
a filtered measure at best.

Strata ships Standard, Complex, Snapshot, LOD, and ad hoc measures, one click
year over year, moving average, and percent of total, and segments at the
measure level. The domain expert builds these in the editor.

</VersusSection>

<VersusSection slug={props.slug} competitor={props.competitor} theme="operator">

Looker's operator is the LookML developer. Business users explore within the
guardrails a developer built, which is real self service up to the edge of the
explore and a ticket beyond it.

In Strata the primary operator is the non technical user. They build reports,
dashboards, agents, and exports across the whole model, with per keystroke
validation, and a layout engine arranges the result. Engineers keep the model
current, and that upkeep is light because there is no explore to author per
question.

</VersusSection>

<VersusSection slug={props.slug} competitor={props.competitor} theme="performance">

Looker speeds things up with persistent derived tables and aggregate awareness,
on one warehouse connection per model. The rollups live inside Looker and the
queries land on that warehouse.

Strata is aggregate aware too, and it federates: a ClickHouse or Druid hot tier
takes the repetitive queries with partition aware routing, and the warehouse
stays the system of record. One measure definition serves every engine, and the
engines are yours to query from any tool.

</VersusSection>

<VersusSection slug={props.slug} competitor={props.competitor} theme="ai">

Gemini in Looker is a conversational layer on top of the explores, so it is as
good as the explore it lands in and as safe as the developer who built it.

Strata's validation loop is the agent API. Agents propose partial queries, get
compatible next steps and structured refusals, and cannot return a semantically
invalid answer.

</VersusSection>

<VersusSection slug={props.slug} competitor={props.competitor} theme="better">

Looker is mature, self contained, and strong on security, and if you have years
of LookML and people who know it, that is a real asset. Moving to Strata is a
re model: your coding agent writes the Strata YAML from the warehouse, but
LookML does not import. If you are all in on Google Cloud, Looker is BigQuery
native and the Strata BigQuery adapter is coming soon. If you embed analytics into
your own product with per customer theming, Looker's embed SDK is the more
mature path.

Strata earns its place when the explore backlog is the bottleneck, when
cross fact questions are hitting merged results, and when you want the same
governed model to serve agents and a hot tier you own.

</VersusSection>
